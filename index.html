Why experts kept leaving UW's grant system for Excel

02 · Quantitative · Online experiment

Should an AI show its answer before or after you decide?

A 100-person experiment on AI-assisted screening. Accuracy was identical across workflows, but showing AI first raised errors of following wrong advice by 67%, while making users look busier.

About

I'm an M.S. student in Human Centered Design & Engineering at the University of Washington, where I research accessible and privacy-aware robots in Maya Cakmak's Human-Centered Robotics Lab, social robots for K-6 classrooms, and digital mental-health tools for teens.

Before UW I studied Creative Media at City University of Hong Kong and did research at Tsinghua and NUS. I've also worked in industry: evaluating LLM agents at Digital China, designing game scenarios at Tencent Games, and co-founding SeeWall, an assistive-technology startup for blind and low-vision people. There I led 50+ interviews and usability tests and shipped an audio-navigation app and an obstacle-avoidance belt, backed by HK$100,000 in seed funding.

The thread through all of it: I like research that has to survive contact with a real product decision. I'm comfortable owning a study end to end and explaining the trade-offs to engineers, designers and PMs.

Look past the headline metric

100% task success hid workarounds. Equal accuracy hid different errors. I go looking for what the summary number misses.

Match the method to the question

Usability tests for "where does it break", experiments for "does this cause that", co-design for "what should exist".

End with a decision

Every study should leave the team with something to build, fix or stop doing, and an owner for it.
