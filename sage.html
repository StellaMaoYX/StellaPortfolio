EvaluativeCase study 01 · Usability testing

100% task success, and still a shadow system in Excel

A usability study of the Award Request List in SAGE, the University of Washington's grant-management system, done with the SAGE product team in UW's Office of Research.

My role
UX researcher on a team of 4: study design, moderation, analysis, readout
Timeline
Jan to Feb 2026
Methods
Heuristic evaluation, moderated usability testing, think-aloud, SUS
Stakeholder
SAGE product and engineering team, UW Office of Research

The problem

Experienced grant managers kept their own spreadsheets to track requests SAGE was built to track. The team had complaints but no evidence of which problems cost the most.

What we did

A heuristic evaluation to find likely issues cheaply, then 5 moderated sessions with expert users across four schools. 100% task success, but a SUS score of 52.

What changed

The product team triaged our recommendations into its backlog with tickets and priorities, and later reached the same root diagnosis in its own independent analysis.

Stage 1 · Planning · Context

The list grant managers use to run their day

Campus grant administrators use SAGE's Award Request List to track Award Setup Requests and Modification Requests, the two workflows that move every research award through review. Earlier feedback had flagged search, status tracking and visibility, but feedback alone couldn't tell the team which problems mattered most, or why experienced people were still building workarounds.

The surprising part came early. All 5 participants completed every task, and 4 of 5 made critical errors along the way. Their mean SUS score was 52, well below the usual benchmark of 68. Task success was measuring how good experts had become at routing around the system. That gap became the core argument of the study.

Stage 1 · Planning · Research questions

Breaking "is it usable?" into five testable questions

RQ1 · Search & filterHow well can users find a specific request with the current search and filters?
RQ2 · StatusHow clearly does the system communicate status and the next required action?
RQ3 · PrioritizationHow do users plan their daily workload from the list?
RQ4 · WorkaroundsWhat do users do outside SAGE to compensate for its limits?
RQ5 · OwnershipHow do users tell their own action items from items waiting on someone else?

Stages 1–2 · Planning and research · Approach

Spend expert hours only on what needs real users

Participants were scarce: a small, credentialed user base with full calendars. So we used a cheap method first to decide what the expensive method should test.

Why this method, not anotherA survey would have told us people were unhappy, which the team already knew. Analytics couldn't show the workarounds that happen outside SAGE. Watching experts do real tasks while thinking aloud was the only way to see where they left the system and why.
  1. Heuristic evaluationScored the interface against Nielsen's 10 heuristics with severity ratings.
  2. Test planTurned the top issues into 3 realistic task scenarios.
  3. RecruitTargeted outreach across four schools; weekly users with 3+ months on SAGE.
  4. 5 sessionsRemote, moderated, ~55 min each, think-aloud plus SUS.
  5. Severity scoringFrequency, impact, persistence and frustration for each issue.
  6. ReadoutRecommendations mapped to the team that owns each fix.
Participants
N = 5 expert users, 1 to 20+ years on the job: Medicine, Computer Science, Education, Engineering
Session
5 min context interview, 30 to 40 min tasks, 10 min post-study questionnaire
Measures
Task completion, error counts (search, navigation, selection), post-task 1 to 5 ratings, SUS
SUS result
52 / 100 mean, well below the commonly used benchmark of 68

Stage 3 · Synthesis · Findings

Three high-severity issues pushed people out of the system

Before the problems: status labels were rated clear (4 of 5 gave 4 or 5 out of 5), and all 5 went straight to Comments & History for context. We weren't asking the team to rebuild SAGE, only to close specific gaps.

High severity

Filters disappear after every click into a record

Opening a request and coming back wiped every applied filter, so each look at a detail meant rebuilding the list. This happens dozens of times a day.

RecommendationKeep filter state on back navigation and support opening records in a new tab.

4/5 affected
"That happens all the time... if I hit the back button, I don't have my list anymore. I have to search it again."P3 · training grants administrator, 20+ years

High severity

Search can't find awards the way people ask about them

PI name search didn't exist in the Awards tab. The PI filter required an exact "Last, First" format with no hint, and slow results had no loading state.

RecommendationAdd PI name search with flexible formats and a visible loading state.

5/5 affected
"The first time it did not give me anything, but then it finally did."P5 · School of Engineering

High severity

Finding one request means visiting a second system

Search accepted only internal application or request IDs, which grant managers rarely have when a PI calls. They looked the ID up in another portal, then came back to search again.

RecommendationAccept PI name and Award ID directly in SAGE search.

4/5 affected
"It's kind of time consuming to have to go back and forth between two different tabs."P2 · grants manager, Computer Science

Moderate severity

No export, so people copy data into spreadsheets by hand

This was the direct mechanism behind the shadow Excel system we set out to explain.

RecommendationCSV or Excel export that respects the current filters.

3/5 keep external sheets
"It would be great if I could download reports... without having to hunt it down and put it in a separate spreadsheet."P3

Stage 5 · Evaluation · Recommendations

Every fix assigned to the team that would build it

A list of problems handed to "the product team" tends to stall. We mapped each recommendation to an owning function so it could become a ticket the same week.

Impact

"Excel is the real tool... BI Portal is an intermediate step."

From the product team's own synthesis, months later

The SAGE product team turned our recommendations into backlog tickets with priority levels. They accepted some, pushed back on others where business constraints differed from our priority calls, and flagged a few for more discovery.

Months later, while planning a larger migration, the team cross-analyzed three independent evaluations, including ours, and reached the same root diagnosis from their own data.

The pushback is part of the result. Research is an input to a decision, and watching a team weigh, accept and contest our findings showed me the study was taken seriously.

Reflection

What I'd keep, and what I'd change

Worked

  • Heuristic evaluation first kept scarce participant time focused.
  • Pairing 100% task success with a SUS of 52 made the gap hard to dismiss.
  • Owner-mapped recommendations moved straight into a backlog.

Limits

  • Only experienced users; novices haven't built workarounds yet.
  • N = 5 supports finding issues, not estimating how often they occur.
  • We didn't time tasks or log error rates systematically.

Next time

  • Add novice users as a comparison group.
  • Grow to 8 to 10 sessions and add time on task.
  • Include edge-case tasks where the system is most likely to break.
